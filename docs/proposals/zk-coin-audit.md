# zk-coin Protocol Audit — Utility Wins for the OttoChain Asset Model

**Status:** research / design. **Date:** 2026-06-16.

This report audits eight live zero-knowledge / privacy systems — Ergo, Midnight, Zcash, Monero, Starknet, Aztec, Penumbra, and Aleo — against the OttoChain asset model and the readable-JLVM ethos.

## Executive summary

The headline finding is that there are only a small handful of genuinely low-hanging utility wins, and they all reduce to *consuming metakit's already-shipped verifier opcodes* (`groth16_verify` / `pmt_verify` / `poseidon`, pinned at `1.8.0-rc.4`) rather than building any novel cryptography. Two stand out as the cleanest grabs: **confidential amounts** (a homomorphic value-commitment replacement for the plaintext `AssetRecord.amount` — the zk twin of the conservation invariant the combiner already enforces) and a **ZkVerify-gated morphism** (a single new `MorphismSpec` variant whose guard is one combiner-resident `groth16_verify` call). The second is the most on-ethos item in the entire audit, because its proof-carrying-compute flavor is *zk-as-integrity, not zk-as-privacy* — it keeps readable JSON-Logic as the contract language and lets zk enter only as a morphism precondition, rooted in the committed-state MPT a light client already checks (PR #164).

On the central question — can OttoChain superset Ergo and Midnight? — the verdict splits. **Ergo is a clean superset on both axes:** the `AssetPolicy` registry strictly extends Ergo's flat box-tokens (adding the typed 5-bit lattice, orthogonal supply policy, typed morphisms, versioned packages, and field-level light-client proofs), Ergo's derived-supply mint is convergent evidence for a choice we already made, and a shielded `AssetPolicy` mode supersets ZeroJoin/stealth in *utility* (a true growing anonymity set plus confidential amounts, versus detectable equal-value 1/2-per-round mixing) using strictly less bespoke crypto. **Midnight is a partial superset:** we match-or-exceed it on the confidential-*asset* subset (confidential amounts, nullifier-based unlinkability, proof-gated transitions, selective disclosure) and beat it decisively on public-tier legibility, but we genuinely cannot — and per ethos should not — chase its client-held-private-state Kachina model or its compile-time information-flow guarantee. Those are a different trust architecture, not a feature gap.

Algebraically, none of the borrowed mechanisms upgrades the asset model's retraction or repairs its obstructed adjunction in general — the obstruction is forgotten decoration, an information-flow fact that zk relocates but cannot un-forget. The one real prize is that homomorphic commitments give a clean *conservation-check* law on the amount group (not on Compose/Decompose), and nullifiers legitimately strengthen the morphisms to one-time partial functions, which is what makes the hidden conservation law sound. Net: strictly more *utility*, the same *algebra*.

The "what we should not do" line is blunt: **do not build a second cryptographic stack or a second VM, and do not hand-roll a single proof.** That means declining Ergo's native sigma-protocol/ring idiom, any Plonkish/Halo2/Cairo proving engine, Penumbra's validator-DKG flow-encryption DEX, Aztec's client-side private execution, and Midnight's Compact compiler + flow-type checker + client-held state; keeping `CalculatedState` public-by-default with shielded as an opt-in variant; and routing every proof through metakit's verifier — the June 2025 SPL ZK-ElGamal soundness break is exactly the hand-rolled-transcript mistake we avoid. Two honesty caveats carry throughout: every shielded/nullifier/ZkVerify mechanism is **proposed, not shipped** (absent from repo and `asset-model.md` today), and metakit's verifier has **no public security audit** — so the word "audited" is struck from any pitch, and a verifier audit is a hard prerequisite before shielded mode guards real value.

## 1. The zk-coin landscape

Privacy and zero-knowledge mean very different things across eight live systems, and the differences are not stylistic — they are choices about *what is hidden*, *by what cryptographic mechanism*, and *what the user pays for the hiding*. It helps to separate two axes that the loose phrase "zk-coin" runs together. The first axis is **what zero-knowledge is doing**: hiding state (confidentiality) versus compressing computation (succinct validity). Validity rollups like **Starknet** are the clean illustration that "ZK" is, at base layer, a misnomer for *integrity* — a single STARK attests that an off-chain state transition was computed correctly, while every account, every input, and all calldata stay public; nothing is confidential unless a later opt-in layer (Starknet's STRK20 privacy pools, an application-level 2026 addition with mainnet assets arriving around May 2026) bolts a commitment/note model on top. The privacy chains use zk for the opposite purpose: to keep amounts, parties, or whole program states off the public ledger while still proving the transition was legal.

The second axis is the **ledger model**, and it sorts the privacy systems into recognizable families. *Privacy-by-default UTXO* (**Monero**) hides everything for everyone, uniformly and mandatorily: amounts via Pedersen commitments, recipients via Diffie-Hellman stealth one-time addresses, and senders via a membership argument — historically a CLSAG ring signature over 16 outputs, and, in the in-progress FCMP++ upgrade (Curve-Trees membership proof, Veridise-reviewed in 2025, mainnet targeted for mid-2026, not yet live), against the entire output set. The cost of mandatory privacy is that there is no selective-disclosure escape hatch and no native multi-asset layer. *Shielded-pool models* (**Zcash**, **Penumbra**) keep a transparent ledger alongside an encrypted pool of UTXO-like "notes," letting value move in and out via shielding/deshielding; Zcash extends this to custom assets with ZSA (per-asset value-commitment base), and Penumbra makes the pool multi-asset and IBC-native, layering a sealed-bid batch DEX on top. *Sigma-protocol eUTXO* (**Ergo**) is a fourth idiom: privacy is expressed as ordinary box-spending logic — ring/threshold signatures and the ZeroJoin mixer are built from two composable Σ-protocols (`proveDlog`, a Schnorr-style proof of knowledge of a discrete log, and `proveDHTuple`, a proof of a Diffie-Hellman tuple under DDH) with no separate shielded ledger and no SNARK toolchain at all.

The richest family is *programmable-privacy* (**Midnight**, **Aztec**, **Aleo**), where privacy is a property of arbitrary contract state and logic, not just payments. The three diverge sharply on architecture. Midnight follows the Kachina model: public state on-chain, private state held *on each user's own machine*, linked by a NIZK proving that a private transition justifies the public delta, with selective disclosure enforced at compile time in the Compact language (`disclose()`, private-by-default). Aztec and Aleo are encrypted-UTXO chains where private functions execute **client-side** (Aztec's PXE; Aleo's snarkVM transitions) and only a succinct proof reaches the network; both use the note-commitment-plus-nullifier mechanism, differing in proof system (Aztec's UltraHonk over a BN254/Grumpkin curve cycle; Aleo's Varuna over BLS12-377). What is hidden in this family is the function itself, not merely the amount — which is precisely the capability OttoChain's readable-JLVM thesis deliberately does *not* pursue, since a contract an agent cannot read defeats the point of legible agreements. A note on proof systems for the careful reader: "Halo2" alone does not imply "no trusted setup." Midnight's `midnight-proofs` is the KZG variant of Halo2, which requires a universal/updatable SRS; Zcash's Orchard and Starknet's STARKs are the genuinely setup-free constructions.

Across these eight, the same handful of building blocks recur, and they are the parts worth lifting into OttoChain's asset model — *as opt-in primitives gated on metakit's existing `groth16_verify`/`pmt_verify`/`poseidon` opcodes, not as novel cryptography we roll ourselves*. The single most-repeated cautionary tale across the research is the June 2025 SPL ZK-ElGamal soundness break (a challenge omitted from the Fiat-Shamir transcript let an attacker forge confidential-balance proofs), which is exactly why the move is "consume an existing verifier," never "hand-roll a range or balance proof."

**Cross-cutting primitives worth stealing:**

- **The note-commitment + nullifier triad** — Zcash, Monero (as the key image), Penumbra, Aztec, and Aleo all converge on it. A spend reveals a deterministic nullifier derived from a secret key plus note-unique data; the network rejects any repeat. The double-spend check is a stateful set-membership test, which in OttoChain belongs **combiner-only** as a graceful `CombineRejected` (CLAUDE.md rule #3), mirroring the asset model's existing `usedNonces` atomic read-then-mark — never in `validateSignedUpdate`, where it would be a TOCTOU block-poisoning hazard.
- **Pedersen value commitments + range proofs for confidential amounts** — Monero, Zcash, Penumbra, Midnight. Because the commitment is additively homomorphic, "Σ inputs − Σ outputs = commitment to zero" is the zk twin of the conservation invariant the asset-model combiner already enforces in cleartext; the range proof (preventing negative/overflow forgery) is the one piece that *must* be delegated to an audited verifier.
- **Per-asset / variable-base value commitments** (Zcash ZSA's Asset Base, Penumbra's V_a) — one shielded mode spanning many `policyId`s, value-balance enforced per asset, rather than a per-asset deployment.
- **Selective-disclosure viewing keys** (Zcash IVK/FVK/OVK, Penumbra's FVK split, Midnight's `disclose()`) — visibility cleanly separated from spend authority. This pairs naturally with OttoChain's public/semi-private/private tiers and the interop functor's `Option[OriginProvenance]`: the same readable JLVM contract runs with a different disclosure dial.
- **Validity proofs for off-chain compute** (Starknet's STARK, the aggregation pattern behind every rollup) — a *non-privacy* use of zk: attest that a heavy or hard-to-express transition was computed correctly off-chain, verify it deterministically, and apply the asserted post-state against the same committed-state MPT root a light client checks. This is arguably more on-ethos than confidential amounts, because integrity, not secrecy, is the readable-JLVM thesis.

A precision note, since this section feeds a design discussion: the specific OttoChain realization sketched against several of these protocols — a "shielded `AssetPolicy` mode," `ZkVerify`-gated morphisms, and a combiner-only nullifier set keyed `nullifier/<hash>` — is a **proposed** direction, not yet present in `docs/proposals/asset-model.md` or in any OttoChain or metakit source. Its factual kernel is solid (the metakit zk opcodes ship in `1.8.0-rc.4`, pinned in `project/Dependencies.scala`; rule #3 and the `CommitKey` projection model are real and codified). But metakit's shielded-transfer verifier and its `Sp1Groth16Verifier` have **no public security audit** on record, so the supporting prose should say "the verifier OttoChain leans on," not "the audited verifier." Treat the shielded-mode mapping as a sound design hypothesis to be specified and reviewed, not as shipped capability.

## 2. Can OttoChain superset Ergo and Midnight?

Short answer: **Ergo — yes, on both axes (assets and privacy), and on privacy we can offer strictly more than it does. Midnight — partially: we superset the confidential-*asset* and proof-gated-state slice and beat it on public-tier legibility, but its client-held-private-state model is a different trust architecture we deliberately do not chase.** The distinction that runs through everything below is **superset in *utility* vs. superset in *mechanism***: we can deliver the user-facing win without copying the underlying machine, and in some cases *should not* copy the machine.

One framing note up front, so the rest reads honestly. The privacy capabilities we cite on our side — a "shielded" AssetPolicy mode, ZkVerify-gated morphisms, a combiner-only nullifier set — are a **proposed extension, not shipped code**. What *is* shipped is the load-bearing dependency: metakit 1.8.0-rc.4 (pinned in `project/Dependencies.scala`) ships the verifier path — `Sp1Groth16Verifier` over vendored Besu alt-bn128, exposed as the `groth16_verify` / `pmt_verify` / `poseidon` JLVM opcodes — plus a Poseidon-commitment + nullifier + Merkle-membership + value-conservation shielded-transfer construction proven 2-in/2-out in SP1. Note metakit is **not** independently security-audited (no audit artifact exists in the repo or publicly); the ethos point is "lean on a *deterministic, reusable* verifier rather than hand-roll one," and the SPL ZK-ElGamal soundness failure (June 2025 — a Fiat-Shamir transcript omission that let an attacker forge proofs) is why. So throughout, "OttoChain can" means "is a low-hanging additive extension on existing primitives," not "exists today."

**A correction worth carrying forward**, because it's the kind of subtle ZK detail that's easy to get wrong and propagate: ZeroJoin's indistinguishability is **not** a Σ-OR over two DH-tuples "`(g,α,u,β)` vs `(g,β,u,α)`." Per the Chepurnoy–Saxena paper (eprint 2020/560), the per-output spend statement (Eq. 1) is `proveDHTuple(g, α, u, β) OR proveDlog(g, β)` — a DH-tuple OR'd with a *DLOG*. The two-DH-tuple OR that does exist (Eq. 3) is Bob's register-well-formedness proof, `(g,u,α,β)` vs `(g,u,β,α)`. Everything else about ZeroJoin is accurate (two Σ-protocols, DDH-based, 1/2 → 1/2ⁿ, non-interactive, coordinator-free). We don't depend on the formula — we're not building ZeroJoin — but if we ever describe it, get it right.

> **Verdict (Ergo): SUPERSET.** OttoChain's AssetPolicy registry is a strict mechanical superset of Ergo's native box-tokens, and a shielded AssetPolicy mode on metakit's Groth16 path supersets ZeroJoin/stealth in *utility* (growing anonymity set + confidential amounts, vs. detectable equal-value 1/2-per-round mixing) using *less* bespoke cryptography. The one thing we deliberately do **not** match is Ergo's native Σ-protocol/ring-signature idiom — for our account/record model that is over-complication, and the SP1+Groth16 path dominates it for our ethos.

### 2b. Midnight

Midnight is a dedicated programmable-privacy L1 (Compact + Kachina + Zswap; proof system is **Halo2/PLONK-ish over KZG**, the `midnight-proofs` crate — *not* Groth16). Its architecture is the **inverse** of ours: private-by-default, with private state held **off-chain on each user's machine** (the Kachina split). Our `CalculatedState` is public-by-default. This is the load-bearing asymmetry for everything below.

This is where the **utility-vs-mechanism** distinction has to be stated bluntly, because it is the honest core of the Midnight answer. ## 3. Utility wins for the OttoChain asset model, ranked by ethos-fit

Six external mechanisms, distilled to concrete extensions of our asset model. Each is scored against the lens (readable JLVM is the thesis; zk is the *enabler*; lean on metakit's `groth16_verify`/`pmt_verify`/`poseidon` opcodes — do **not** roll novel ZK; determinism + committed-state-MPT light-client provability are first-class; stateful checks are combiner-only). The ranking is ethos-fit × utility.

A note on what we are buying. metakit 1.8.0-rc.4 already ships the *verifier* half of a Zcash-style shielded transfer — Poseidon note-commitments, fixed-depth Poseidon-Merkle membership, nullifier semantics, value conservation, proven 2-in/2-out in SP1, wrapped to Groth16-BN254, and verified in the pure-JVM `Sp1Groth16Verifier` (vendored Besu alt-bn128, no native deps), exposed as JLVM opcodes. **Caveat that changes the framing: there is no public security audit of metakit or its `Sp1Groth16Verifier`.** "Lean on metakit's verifier instead of hand-rolling" still holds — a deterministic JVM Groth16 verifier consuming SP1-produced proofs is the right shape, and the SPL ZK-ElGamal break (June 2025, a Fiat-Shamir transcript omission that let an attacker mint confidential tokens) is precisely the failure mode we avoid by *not* writing our own sigma-protocol verifier. But "audited" must be struck from any internal pitch, and a metakit verifier audit is a prerequisite before a shielded mode touches real value. Equally: none of `nullifier`, `shielded`, or `ZkVerify` exists in OttoChain source today. Everything below is a *proposed* additive variant on the existing sealed traits, not an existing plan.

### P0 — Confidential amounts via homomorphic value-commitments + a metakit-verified range proof

**Sources:** Monero RingCT (`C = aH + bG` + Bulletproofs+), Zcash Sapling (Pedersen value commitment + binding signature), Penumbra (per-asset generators `V_a = H(a)`).

**User utility:** on-chain amounts become hidden; an observer sees a commitment, not a `Long`. Balances cannot be aggregated; transfer values are private.

**Mechanism on our side:** replace the plaintext `AssetRecord.amount` with a Pedersen/Poseidon commitment in a "shielded" `RegistryShape.AssetPolicy` flavor. Our **derived-supply conservation invariant** — `Σ(record.amount where policyId == p)` with `totalMinted` cached-not-counter (§3 of the asset model) — is the *exact* plaintext twin of the homomorphic balance equation `Σ C_in − Σ C_out − C_mint/burn = commitment-to-0`. The combiner already enforces conservation arithmetically; the shielded mode swaps the cleartext sum for a commitment sum-to-zero plus a metakit-verified range proof (the part that stops negative/overflow forgery — the one place we must NOT roll our own circuit). Penumbra's per-asset value generator `V_policyId` is the clean way to make this multi-policy: our canonical `policyId` *is* the natural asset-base handle.

**Verdict: [LOW-HANGING — with a moderate sub-edge].** Confidential amounts are an *upgrade of an invariant the combiner already computes*, not a new subsystem. Range-proof verification is deterministic → combiner-resident; commitments commit into the existing committed-state MPT root → light-client provable. The honest sub-edge that keeps it from "trivial": conservation and `derived totalMinted` are currently load-bearing *plaintext* checks, so re-expressing them as commitment arithmetic is real surgery on the combiner's hottest invariant. Ship it as the *first* shielded primitive, but as a deliberate phase, not a one-line flag.

### P0 — A ZkVerify-gated (proof-carrying) morphism

**Sources:** Aztec (`ZkGated` notes), Midnight (Compact predicate proofs), Aleo (transition proofs), Starknet (validity-proof pattern, *orthogonal to privacy*).

**User utility:** two distinct wins from one primitive. (a) A morphism whose guard is "this proof verifies" — confidential/predicate-gated `Transfer`/`Compose` ("balance < limit", "KYC passed", "is a member") without revealing the witness. (b) **Proof-carrying off-chain compute** (the Starknet idea, no privacy required): a batch `Compose`/`Decompose`, or a `Fractionalize` codomain too expensive to recompute in-combiner, is computed off-chain and *attested* — the combiner verifies a Groth16 proof and applies the asserted post-state instead of re-executing, exactly as Starknet's L1 verifier accepts a state-root transition.

**Mechanism on our side:** a new `MorphismVisibility` / `ZkGated` `MorphismSpec` on the existing typed morphism graph. The payload carries an SP1 proof + public inputs; the combiner calls metakit's `groth16_verify` and gracefully `CombineRejected`s on failure. This rides the existing three-layer validation verbatim — L1 structural on `AssetCommit` bits, stateful verify in the combiner, JLVM guard on top — with **zero spine reshaping**. The attested-transition variant rhymes perfectly with our committed-state MPT: the proof attests a transition of the committed calc-state tree, and light clients verify against the same root that PR #164 already merged.

**Verdict: [LOW-HANGING].** It reduces to *one* call to a metakit opcode inside the combiner. Determinism holds (`verify(proof, publicInputs) → bool`); it is one additive `MorphismSpec` variant; it changes no consensus surface. This is the single most ethos-true item in the audit because the proof-carrying-compute flavor is *zk-as-integrity, not zk-as-privacy* — it keeps readable JLVM as the contract language and lets zk enter only as a morphism precondition. It is also the natural realization of open-Q2 (registerable custom morphisms): a `ZkGated` morphism is just another registry-package entry.

### P1 — A nullifier set for unlinkable / single-use transfer

**Sources:** Zcash/Penumbra/Aztec/Aleo (commitment-tree leaf + revealed nullifier), Monero (key image as deterministic nullifier).

**User utility:** transfer-graph unlinkability and one-time-spend semantics — a spent note cannot be linked to its commitment, so the custody graph is private; double-spends are rejected by a uniqueness check.

**Mechanism on our side:** spend = reveal a nullifier + a Poseidon-Merkle membership proof of a note commitment (the `pmt_verify`/`poseidon` opcodes already exist). The **nullifier set is a new combiner-only state map** — `CommitKey nullifier/<hash>` — exposed as a **TOTAL committed-state projection** like `asset/` and `nonce/`, so it inherits field-level light-client proofs from the merged committed-state root. Double-spend rejection is an atomic read-then-mark inside a single insert, signature-tiebreaking the loser as `CombineRejected` — structurally identical to the asset model's existing `usedNonces` commit-reveal discipline (R18). This is the textbook case for **CLAUDE.md rule #3**: a nullifier-membership read in `validateSignedUpdate` would be a TOCTOU block-poisoning hazard (concurrent spend → `Invalid` → the whole block drops for every tx), so the check lives *only* in the combiner.

**Verdict: [LOW-HANGING].** The membership opcode exists, the set is a SortedSet in `CalculatedState`, and the double-spend gate is the same combiner-only pattern we already designed for nonces. Pairs naturally with P0 (confidential amounts + nullifier = a real shielded transfer). Ranked P1 only because, standalone, it delivers unlinkability without amount-hiding — most users want both, so it earns its keep stacked on P0.

### P1 — Selective-disclosure viewing keys

**Sources:** Zcash IVK/FVK/OVK (ZIP 310/316), Midnight `disclose()` + regulator viewing keys, Penumbra FVK/IVK/OVK, Starknet STRK20 encrypted viewing keys.

**User utility:** auditability-with-privacy — a holder hands an auditor a read-only key that reveals shielded amounts/parties *without conferring spend authority*. This is the regulator-aligned escape hatch that mandatory-privacy systems (Monero) structurally cannot offer, and it fits our "residual trust / receipts" thesis directly.

**Mechanism on our side:** model a holder-granted view capability on the FVK/IVK/OVK split, and pair it with the interop functor's `Option[OriginProvenance]` and with reputation/attestation signals for compliant disclosure. On the public OttoWeb tier this is free (state is already readable); it is load-bearing only *over* a shielded policy, where it gates who can open commitments.

**Verdict: [MODERATE].** The crypto is conceptually clean (a key that decrypts but cannot sign) and on-ethos. It is P1-not-P0 because it is only meaningful *after* P0/P1-nullifier ship — there is nothing to selectively disclose until amounts/links are hidden — and because a real key hierarchy (spend vs view separation, diversified addresses) is more than a flag. Sequence it as the third cut, bundled with the shielded mode it audits.

We get strictly more privacy for *less* bespoke cryptography by reusing metakit. Ergo's native sigma-protocol/ring-signature idiom is the one capability we should deliberately NOT mirror.

## 4. Algebraic note: does any of this upgrade our retraction?

Short answer: the three external mechanisms each touch a *different* coordinate of our algebra, and **none of them upgrade the retraction or repair the adjunction in general** — but one of them (homomorphic value commitments) does give us a genuinely cleaner law on a *new, narrower* object, and that is the honest prize. The obstruction to a clean two-sided inverse / Galois connection is **forgotten decoration** (`decimals`, royalty terms, off-chain metadata — §4.3, §6.2), and forgotten decoration is an information-flow fact, not a cryptographic one. zk hides bits; it does not un-forget them. So the default posture is: zk changes *who can see* a coordinate, almost never *whether the coordinate exists*.

**(a) Homomorphic value commitments — a real isomorphism, but on the amount group, not on Compose/Decompose.** Pedersen's `commit(a) + commit(b) = commit(a+b)` is a bona fide **monoid homomorphism** from `(Amounts, +)` into the commitment group `(G, +)`, and (binding modulo the dlog assumption) it is *injective on the value/blinding pair*, i.e. an isomorphism onto its image. That genuinely upgrades **one** of our laws: today the conservation invariant `Σ inputs = Σ outputs ± mint/burn` is a plaintext equality the combiner re-adds; under a shielded `AssetPolicy` it becomes a commitment equation `Σ C_in − Σ C_out = commit(±mintburn)` checkable *without opening the amounts* — the homomorphic image of an invariant we already enforce. But note precisely what this is and isn't. It makes the **amount** functor (asset → its value) a homomorphism into a group where the conservation law is preserved on the nose. It does **not** make `Compose`/`Decompose` an isomorphism: our `Compose` is **multiset-union of component *ids*** with a `meet` on the behavior lattice — neither of those is the amount-addition Pedersen linearizes. A confidential basket would carry a confidential *sum* of component amounts, but `Decompose` still needs the **stored `ComponentWitness` list** (the faithful-retraction reveal, §4 fact 1) to restore each part's behavior/holder/binding, because addition is lossy: `commit(a+b)` does not factor back into `commit(a), commit(b)`. So Pedersen converts the **conservation check** to a homomorphic-and-hidden form (a clean win), but the **decomposition** stays a stored-witness retraction. The aggregation monoid does not become a group just because amounts commit homomorphically.

**(b) Shield / unshield — an adjunction *shape*, and it neither removes nor adds the obstruction; it just hides which side you're on.** Transparent↔shielded (Zcash, Aztec, Penumbra, Aleo all expose exactly this pair) is structurally the **same retraction we already have for `Wrap`/`Unwrap` and `Compose`/`Decompose`**: `shield` then `unshield` restores the value because the shielded note *stores the amount and owner verbatim under a commitment*, exactly as the custody fiber stores the origin claim verbatim (§6.1). `unshield ∘ shield = id` on value; `shield ∘ unshield` is *not* claimed as an identity on the shielded side. Critically, **zk does not remove the "forgotten decoration" obstruction the interop functor `F` has — it relocates it.** Going transparent→shielded *adds* a forgetful step (the public ledger forgets the amount and the linkage), and going shielded→transparent reveals only what the holder discloses. The decoration `F` already dropped (`decimals`, royalties) is just as unrecoverable inside a shielded pool as outside it; we have merely layered confidentiality on top of the *same* lax/partial/forgetful map. So shield/unshield is honestly described with our existing §6.2 verdict: **adjunction-shaped, obstructed on the forgetful coordinates, a left adjoint only after quotienting out the hidden/forgotten data** — `Otto/≅_decoration` now also quotients "publicly-visible vs shielded." It is not a Galois connection and we should not claim one. The one *new* thing it buys, algebraically, is that **selective disclosure makes the quotient a holder-controlled dial** (a viewing-key/FVK choosing how much of `≅_decoration` to collapse) rather than a fixed forgetful map — useful, but a refinement of the obstruction, not its removal.

**(c) Nullifiers — these *do* strengthen a law, and it's the cleanest categorical win of the three.** A nullifier set makes each note **linear / one-time**: a note can be consumed *at most once*, enforced by an insert-if-absent (non-membership-then-mark) check on a committed set. That is exactly the **affine/linear-resource discipline** that our `usedNonces` linear set already imposes on `AuthorizeCompose` (R18) — "consumed exactly once, signature-tiebreak the loser." Algebraically this upgrades the relevant morphisms from "a relation you could in principle replay" to **partial functions with disjoint, single-use domains** — i.e. it enforces that shielded `Transfer`/`Decompose`/`Unshield` are honest-to-goodness *moves of a resource*, not copyable rewrites. This is the property that makes the conservation monoid trustworthy under confidentiality: without one-time spends, `commit(a+b)=commit(a+b)` would let you spend the same hidden note twice and still "balance." So the nullifier is what lets (a)'s homomorphic conservation law actually mean conservation. It does **not**, however, touch the retraction or the adjunction — it is orthogonal: it strengthens *spend-once* on the morphisms, while the retraction is about *restore-faithfully* on the objects. Both are stored-witness disciplines; they compose cleanly but neither implies the other.

**Net, stated honestly.** The deep obstruction — that import/export and Compose/Decompose are retractions/obstructed-adjunctions rather than clean inverses/Galois connections — is **information-theoretic (forgotten structure)**, and the external zk machinery is **about confidentiality and single-use, which are orthogonal axes**. So: (i) we get a *real* homomorphism/isomorphism, but on the **amount/value group** via Pedersen, where it upgrades the **conservation check**, not on the aggregation monoid where the retraction lives; (ii) shield/unshield is the same obstructed-adjunction shape we already documented, with the holder-controlled quotient as the only genuine refinement; (iii) nullifiers legitimately strengthen the **morphisms** to linear/one-time partial functions, which is what makes the hidden conservation law sound. The aspirational over-reach to avoid is asserting that confidential amounts make Compose/Decompose a group isomorphism, or that zk turns `F ⊣ G` into a clean adjunction — neither follows, and both would be the same overclaim discipline we already rejected for "monoidal category" and "Galois connection." The model that survives is unchanged in shape: **stored-witness retraction + obstructed adjunction**, now with a homomorphically-checkable, confidential conservation law and one-time spends bolted onto the morphisms — strictly more *utility*, the same *algebra*.
